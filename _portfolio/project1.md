The PressMate started as a progect for the Product Realization class at BU (ME 537). The goal is to go from concept to 10 fully working prototypes, and then a mass production plan for 10,000 units. The PressMate started as question; what if the AeroPress was inverted and could be drank straignt out of? From here, we drew a basic design and and made four mockups to test our design

Add mockup pictures here

We cemented our design with the PVC mockup (right most one in picture above). It consists of a vessel and a plunger that fits inside. The plunger has a mesh bottom to filter coffee grounds, and an o-ring that seals it the the vessel as it moves down. It works by filling the vessel with hot water and coffee grounds, and then the user inserts the plunger and slowly pushes it down to push the coffee grounds to the bottom of the vessel. The brewed coffee comes up through the filter and fills the plunger. The user is then able to drink straight from the plunger by holding the vessel.

However, it is unsafe to drink from PVC plastic, espesilly when it is heated. Furthermore, we wanted a premium design that is nicer that of the AeroPress' plastic design. We decided to build the entire coffee press out of steel, and we came up with the name, the PressMate. From here, we built our first prototype, an engineering validation test (EVT) to see if we would be able to build it out of steel

With the EVT, we learned some hard lessons. It was designed to have a grove in the bottom where the o-ring would sit. The grove would be cut into the steel plate that was welded onto the bottom of the vessel using a lathe. However, we learned that welding hardened the carbon steel, and broke the groving tool we were using, while also destroying our first EVT prototype.

Dented EVT pic here

Switching to a carbide grovong tool fixed this problem, and we were able to make one good vessel. A coozie was also make to insulate the press from burning a user's hand, and a lid was added to make sure coffee would not spill when being pressed. But, sealing is another major problem we encountered. First The o-rings we choose for the EVT was far too flimsy, and did not stay in the goove, we had to stuff two o-rings in the groove to make them stay and seal.

EVT pics

However, the EVT was too small, and its quality wasn't great; the finish was poor and the welds looked bad. We wanted to improve this for the DVT, so we increased the vessel from 2" to 2.9" in diameter, and increased its height from 3" to 3.75". We also seasoned the entire press with grape seed oil to make it food safe, determined that a lid was not needed, and added a silicone lip around the top of the plunger to protect the user's lips from getting burnt as they drink coffee.

DVT Pics

3 total EVTs were made. They successfully made a cup of coffee (It was pretty good!), sealed perfectly, and cemented our overall design. This doesn't mean we did not have our challenges though. The plunger and vessels are made of steel pipe
